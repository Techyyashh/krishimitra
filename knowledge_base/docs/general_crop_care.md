## Integrated Pest Management (IPM)
IPM means using many methods together and using chemicals only when needed. Steps: grow healthy
seed and resistant varieties, rotate crops, remove infected plants and weeds, use traps (yellow
sticky traps for whiteflies and aphids, pheromone traps for moths), protect friendly insects such
as lady beetles and spiders, and spray chemicals only when pest numbers cross the economic threshold.

## When to spray
Spray in the early morning or late evening when there is little wind. Do not spray before rain.
Do not spray during flowering if bees are active. Always read and follow the dose on the product
label. Never mix different pesticides unless the label or KVK says it is safe.

## Safety while spraying
Wear gloves, a mask, full-sleeve clothes and closed shoes. Do not eat, drink or smoke while spraying.
Wash hands and body with soap after spraying. Keep children and animals away. Store pesticides locked,
in the original container, away from food. Never reuse empty pesticide containers for water or food.
If someone feels dizzy, vomits or has breathing problems after spraying, take them to a doctor or the
nearest hospital immediately and carry the pesticide container with you.

## Waiting period before harvest
Every pesticide has a waiting period (pre-harvest interval) written on the label. Do not harvest or
sell vegetables and fruits before this period is over.

## Organic options
Neem oil (azadirachtin) helps against sucking pests and some fungi. Trichoderma and Pseudomonas
fluorescens are friendly microbes used for seed treatment and soil application against soil-borne
diseases. Well-decomposed compost and crop rotation improve soil health and reduce disease.

## Watering and disease
Most leaf diseases spread when leaves stay wet for long. Prefer drip or furrow irrigation over
sprinklers, water in the morning, and avoid water-logging. Good spacing and pruning let air move and
leaves dry faster.
